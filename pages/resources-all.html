Complete Resume Building Guide

Everything you need to create a standout resume

Build a Resume That Gets Noticed

Your resume is often the first impression an employer has of you. A well-crafted resume highlights your skills, experience, and accomplishments in a clear, professional format.

Resume Essentials

  • Contact Information: Full name, professional email, phone number, LinkedIn URL, and city/state
  • Summary or Objective: 2–3 sentences highlighting your goals and top qualifications
  • Education: Degree, major, university, graduation date, and GPA (if 3.0+)
  • Experience: Relevant jobs, internships, and volunteer work with bullet-point accomplishments
  • Skills: Technical and soft skills relevant to the roles you are targeting

Action Verbs to Use

  • Analyzed, Developed, Implemented, Managed, Created, Led, Collaborated, Increased, Reduced, Presented
Quantify Your Impact

Instead of "Helped increase sales," write "Contributed to a 15% increase in quarterly sales by developing a new client outreach strategy." Numbers make your accomplishments concrete and memorable.

Cover Letter Writing Guide

Make a compelling case for why you are the perfect fit

Why Cover Letters Matter

A cover letter lets you tell your story beyond the resume. It connects your experience to the specific role and shows genuine interest in the company.

Cover Letter Structure

  1. Opening paragraph: State the role you are applying for and why you are excited about it
  2. Body (1–2 paragraphs): Connect 2–3 of your strongest experiences or skills to the job requirements
  3. Closing paragraph: Express enthusiasm, mention a follow-up, and thank the reader
Customize Every Time

A generic cover letter is worse than none at all. Reference the company by name, mention a specific project or value of theirs, and explain why this particular role is the right fit for you right now.

LinkedIn Profile Optimization

Build your professional brand and get noticed by recruiters

Make Your Profile Work for You

Recruiters spend an average of 7 seconds reviewing a LinkedIn profile. A complete, keyword-rich profile dramatically increases your visibility and connection requests.

Profile Checklist

  • Professional headshot (no filters, good lighting, plain background)
  • Compelling headline — more than just your job title
  • Summary that tells your story in 3–5 sentences
  • All relevant experience with bullet-point accomplishments
  • Education section with activities and societies
  • Skills section with at least 5 endorsed skills
  • Personalized URL (e.g., linkedin.com/in/yourname)
  • At least 50 connections

Interview Preparation

Walk in confident and walk out with an offer

The STAR Method

Use the STAR method to structure answers to behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time when…"):

  • Situation: Set the scene and context
  • Task: Describe your responsibility or challenge
  • Action: Explain the specific steps you took
  • Result: Share the outcome, ideally with numbers

Common Interview Questions to Prepare

  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here?
  • What is your greatest strength / weakness?
  • Describe a challenge you overcame
  • Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
  • Do you have any questions for us?
Research Before Every Interview

Read the company's mission, recent news, and the job description thoroughly. Prepare 3–5 thoughtful questions to ask at the end — interviewers remember candidates who ask good questions.

Professional Dress Guide

Dress for the job you want, not the job you have

Business Professional vs. Business Casual

Business Professional

Appropriate for: Formal interviews, Big 4 recruiting events, finance and law firms

  • Tailored suit (navy, charcoal, or black)
  • Dress shirt or blouse (white or light colors)
  • Conservative tie (for men)
  • Closed-toe dress shoes (polished)
  • Minimal jewelry and accessories

Business Casual

Appropriate for: Career fairs, campus interviews, most internship day-to-day

  • Slacks or khakis (no jeans)
  • Collared shirt, blouse, or sweater
  • Clean, professional shoes
  • No athletic wear, graphic tees, or flip-flops
When in Doubt, Overdress

It is always better to be slightly overdressed than underdressed for an interview. You can always dress down for casual days once you are hired and have observed the office culture.
